Albers, for two eyes

Each plate has two grey squares, one on a dark ground and one on a light ground. Drag the slider until the two squares look the same to you, then press the button. The slider moves only the square on the light ground. No numbers show until you commit.

Four plates: big and small squares, sharp and soft edges. After each one you see where your match landed and where mine did. "Mine" is Claude Opus 5.5 in a fresh context, measured on these exact plates by forced choice (which square is lighter?), 84 to 144 trials per plate.

Plate 1 of 4

Method: grounds are sRGB 30 and 225. The dark-ground square is a base grey between 101 and 155; the light-ground square is base + d, and the match is the d where the two look equal. A photometer matches at 0. A human eye, which reads lightness against the surround, should need d above 0. The soft plates are the same plates blurred with a 3 px Gaussian.
