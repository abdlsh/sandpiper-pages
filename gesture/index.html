Two forces at once

Kandinsky built every line out of forces acting on a point. One force gives a straight line, two forces taking turns give an angle, and two forces acting together give a curve. Three days ago I implemented that literally, as a small Forth in which each force finishes before the next begins. In principle it can draw any curve. But when I wrote his gestures from his descriptions, they came out as timid meanders. A hand doesn't work in sequence. Its strokes overlap in time and their speeds add.

1. One parameter, three kinds of line

Two straight strokes of equal length, one pointing up and one pointing right. Each has the bell-shaped speed profile of a real hand movement (Plamondon's lognormal). The only thing that changes is how long the second stroke waits after the first.

delay

Right: the speed of each stroke over time (grey) and the speed of the point (black). With no overlap the point stops dead between the strokes and turns on the spot. With full overlap the two strokes become one force, pulling diagonally.

2. His figures as aims

Each stroke aims at a point (dotted) and starts before the previous one is finished, so the line cuts each corner and reaches none of its points except the last. Pull the slider left and the strokes run in sequence, so the line becomes arcs meeting at corners, which is what my Forth could do. Push it right and they fuse into one sweep, and the small features disappear.

sequence fused at quarter speed: watch it slow in the curves



3. Seven aims


Seven points, and one line aimed at each in turn. A careful hand reaches every point and turns sharply at each. A hurried one only reaches the last, and the points it was aimed at float free beside it.

What this didn't fix


Before looking at his Figs. 42 and 44, I wrote both from his text in this new language. They came out wrong the same way my Forth versions did three days earlier: for 42 I drew four even humps where he drew one big one, one small one and a long calm tail. The overlapping strokes made his gestures easy to rebuild once I'd seen them, in four or five tries each. They didn't make me read the gestures better from the words. That part was me.
