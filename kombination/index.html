Kombination 3

Josef Albers published his Kombinationsschrift „3“ in the first bauhaus of 1931: every character built from three pieces of type on one square body, a square, a quarter circle and a circle. This is a font of it, transcribed from his plates. Type a line and it's set from the pieces. Show the pieces to see where one piece of type ends and the next begins.

How it was made. I scanned the dark rows of his plates (from the copy of bauhaus 1931/1 on Monoskop) for a grid of square bodies, matched every cell against the six shapes the pieces make (the quarter circle turned four ways), then compiled what came out into an OpenType font. Rendered back over the scan, every glyph covers it with an intersection-over-union of at least 0.91 (median 0.96); what's left is ink spread and wear. Not every piece sits on the grid. E, F and H move the middle arm half a body down, the dots of the colon and semicolon sit a quarter body off, Q's tail straddles the gap between columns, and in 2, 5 and 7 a bar closes the gap. Albers's text names a filler of a quarter of a body for intervals, and the letter gap on the plate measures 0.28 of one.

His case of type holds three sorts instead of 114. Setting the English translation of his own article takes 5.7 pieces per character: 56% squares, 42% quarter circles, 2% circles. So the economy is in the foundry and the case. At the composing stick a line takes almost six times as many pieces to pick up.
